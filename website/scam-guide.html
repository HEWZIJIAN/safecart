How to Spot a Scam

Fake stores, phishing, payment traps — they all share the same fingerprints. Learn the six-second gut check once, and you'll feel the wrongness before you ever type your card.
The six-second gut check
  1. 1

    Too good to be true

    A $600 item for $89 isn't luck. If the deal is impossible, it's bait.

  2. 2

    Manufactured urgency

    Countdown timers and "only 2 left" exist to stop you thinking.

  3. 3

    The address is off

    paypa1, not paypal. Read every domain letter by letter.

  4. 4

    Brand-new website

    Registered days ago, no reviews, no history. A free whois lookup shows its age.

  5. 5

    Unusual payment

    Gift cards, wire transfer, crypto, or a "release fee" — walk away.

  6. 6

    You didn't ask for it

    An out-of-the-blue text or email pushing you to act right now — that pressure is the scam.

The usual suspects
Fake online store
Bait: a huge discount on a hot product.  Tell: lookalike domain, fake countdown, no real reviews.
"Redelivery fee" text
Bait: "$1.99 to release your parcel."  Tell: carriers never text you for money.
"Payment failed" email
Bait: "update your card or lose access."  Tell: real brands don't fix billing through an email link.
"You've been paid"
Bait: "confirm your card to release the funds."  Tell: money coming IN never needs your card.
Lookalike login
Bait: "verify your account now."  Tell: read the domain — amaz0n, paypa1, g00gle.
Gift-card demand
Bait: "settle your bill with a gift card."  Tell: no real company or agency EVER takes gift cards.
If you remember one thing
When something rushes you to pay or click — slow down. Open the app or type the real address yourself, and never pay by gift card.
